----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
METHOD, AND WHY TWO NUMBERS ARE GIVEN FOR PITT
----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Interviewer speech is the *INV: tier of the CHAT transcript. Participant speech is *PAR:.
A window is the [start, end] interval actually used to cut the scored clip. For each window I take
every *INV: utterance, clip it to the window, and union the clipped intervals, so two interviewer
turns that overlap are not counted twice.

Pitt is measured TWICE because the Pitt transcripts are not fully timed:
  6589 *PAR: and 1683 *INV: utterances carry a time bullet; 537 *PAR: and 795 *INV: do not.
  LOWER BOUND  = union of TIMED *INV: intervals only. Every second of it is on disk. Untimed
                 interviewer turns contribute zero, which is why it is a floor and not an estimate.
  UPPER BOUND  = the lower bound plus, for each UNTIMED *INV: utterance, the whole gap between its
                 timed neighbours, clipped to the window. That gap is the widest the turn could
                 possibly be, so this is a ceiling. The truth is between the two.
ADReSS-2020 is fully timed (0 untimed utterances in all 156 files), so its numbers are exact and the
two bounds coincide.
ADReSSo has no timed transcript at all, so it gets neither. See its section.

NOT USED: pitt_conflict_manifest.csv column inv_ms_in_window. It is 0.0000 on all 468 rows because
the regex behind it requires a time bullet on the *INV: line and 795 Pitt *INV: lines have none.
It is a known artefact (DISCREPANCIES.md D4) and it is not cited anywhere below.

====================================================================================================
DECISION RULE
====================================================================================================
Rule as given: if the MEDIAN interviewer share of the window is under 15 percent, conclude
'no recut needed, sensitivity check not triggered'. If over 15 percent, the recut sensitivity
check is TRIGGERED for that set. PART 5 does not perform the recut either way.

Median interviewer share of the scored window, per set:
  Pitt (468)        0.0000  lower bound   |  0.0000  upper bound   -> UNDER 0.1500 on both
  Pitt (708, all tertiles)  0.0000  lower bound                       -> UNDER 0.1500
  ADReSS-2020 (156) 0.0501  exact                                     -> UNDER 0.1500
  ADReSSo (237)     NOT MEASURABLE, no timed transcript exists              -> rule cannot be applied

Means, for completeness, because the median hides the tail:
  Pitt (468)        mean share 0.0209 lower, 0.0688 upper
  ADReSS-2020 (156) mean share 0.0853

VERDICT
  Pitt          NO RECUT NEEDED, SENSITIVITY CHECK NOT TRIGGERED. Median share 0.0000 (lower)
                and 0.0000 (upper), both far under 0.1500. 13 of 468 windows hold more
                than 5 s of interviewer speech on the lower bound, 75 of 468 on the upper.
  ADReSS-2020   NO RECUT NEEDED, SENSITIVITY CHECK NOT TRIGGERED. Median share 0.0501, exact,
                under 0.1500. 22 of 156 windows hold more than 5 s of interviewer speech.
  ADReSSo       RULE CANNOT BE APPLIED. There is no timed transcript for this set, so the median
                interviewer share does not exist. No recut is triggered on a set that cannot be
                measured, and it is not cleared either. What is on the record instead is the ASR
                lower bound: 10 of 237 clips still contain an examiner prompt phrase,
                4 of them among the 90 windows that start at 0.0 s.

  No set crosses the 15 percent median line, so on the rule as written the sensitivity check is
  NOT TRIGGERED and no recut is performed. Nothing in Table 1 changes from PART 5.

  One honest caveat, stated because it matters and not because it changes the verdict. The median
  is under the line on every measurable set, but the median is 0.0000 on Pitt precisely because 255
  of 468 windows have no interviewer in them at all. The interviewer sits in a minority of windows
  and sits there heavily: the worst Pitt window is 12.8170 s of interviewer inside a
  38.7400 s window (Dem 173-1, share 0.3308); the worst ADReSS-2020 window is
  17.7990 s inside 30.0000 s (S139, share 0.5933). 35 windows across the
  two sets carry more than 5 s. A median-based rule passes those windows; a per-window rule would
  not. That is a property of the rule, not a finding about the data, and the rule is the one given.
